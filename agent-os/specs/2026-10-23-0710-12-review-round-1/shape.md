# PR #12 review round 1 (launch 2026-10-23-0710) — Shaping Notes

## Scope

The nine findings of Redline round 1 on PR #12 (`/redline:setup`, second half of #6). They are in `redline/round-1-findings.json`, and the review was posted to the PR.

- **R1-1** · correctness · medium · A Redline round here that fixes `checks-template.sh` or the generator's output can never pass gate step 7 (decided by the person: (a) defer them).
- **R1-2** · nit · README says every writing subcommand takes `--dry-run`, but `checks` has none.
- **R1-3** · correctness · low · The settings merge overwrites existing values and can build a mixed marketplace source.
- **R1-4** · nit · Rewriting `settings.json` drops CRLF, against the standard this PR adds.
- **R1-5** · correctness · low · `config --force` throws away every hand-tuned key to update checker and stall (decided by the person: (a) update only the derived keys, reviewer kept).
- **R1-6** · nit · `checks.json` text fields other than `cmd`/`source` are not checked for newlines or quotes.
- **R1-7** · correctness · low · `version` treats a newer `checks.sh` as older and tells the person to downgrade it.
- **R1-8** · correctness · low · The new version precheck triggers a permission prompt on every `/redline:redline` launch (decided by the person: (a) a narrow allow rule).
- **R1-9** · nit · `sed -i ''` only works with BSD sed.

Scope is these findings and nothing more.

### Judgment calls

The shaper returned these three to the person; the person decided each on 2026-10-23, and the decisions are recorded above. The question, story, options and recommendation below are what the person saw, kept as written.

- **R1-1: Template and generator fixes in a self-hosted round.**
  - The question: with the dogfood case in gate step 7, a round here that edits `scripts/checks-template.sh`'s body or the text `redline-setup.sh checks` writes cannot pass, and no role can regenerate `checks.sh`. Should such findings be deferred to a person, should the dogfood case compare less, or should those rounds be allowed to fail?
  - Story: a later Redline round on this repository finds a bug in the template's `run_check`, as #7's round did. The shaper follows `guard.md` and plans the fix in `scripts/checks-template.sh`; the implementer makes it. The checker runs `checks.sh`, and `setup` fails on "generated from checks.json and stamped, it equals the committed checks.sh", because the regenerated file now carries the new template lines and the committed one does not. No role may regenerate it, so every repair pass fails the same way and the round ends in an error or at the cap, with a fix that was right.
  - Why it goes to the person: a new standard. It decides what a run may change in this repository, and `guard.md:9` currently says the opposite of what the gate allows.
  - Options:
    - (a) Defer them. Cost: point 11 of `.claude/commands/agent-os/shape-spec.md` (outside the gate, so the hash does not move), `agent-os/standards/scripts/guard.md:9` (through the retrospective) and a `README.md` Known limits line say that here a finding whose fix touches `scripts/checks-template.sh` below its header, or changes the text `redline-setup.sh checks` writes for this repository's `checks.json`, is `defer`: the round records the intended lines in the spec's Deferred entry, and a person makes the change, regenerates `checks.sh` and restamps after the run. No code. Effect: the dogfood check stays whole; template work in a self-hosted round moves to a person.
    - (b) Narrow the dogfood case. Cost: about ten lines in `tests/setup-test.sh:345-353` to compare only the header and the check block (the parts that come from `checks.json`), the `testing/tests.md` bullets and the README Files row. Effect: a run can fix the template's functions again; `checks.sh` here can run stale functions with nothing noticing until a person regenerates; a change to the generator's own header or block text still fails, so those findings still need (a)'s rule.
    - (c) Accept. Cost: none, or a Known limits line. Effect: such rounds fail predictably until a person regenerates.
  - Shaper's recommendation: (a). It keeps the drift check whole, costs only text, and treats the template the way this repository already treats `checks.sh`, the guard and the hook: a person changes Redline's own gate machinery after the run.
  - Decided: (a) Defer them, 2026-10-23, by @michaelfromtheoutfit (see Decisions).
- **R1-5: What `config` does to an existing `redline.json`.**
  - The question: a rerun can only take the new `checker` and `checkerStallMs` with `--force`, which also resets every hand-tuned key. Should `config` update only the keys it derives, or keep the all-or-nothing reset and say so?
  - Story: a teammate reruns `/redline:setup` on a repository whose suite got faster, so the baseline drops under five minutes and `checker` should become `full` with a shorter stall. Say someone had set `maxRounds` to 5 and a reviewer effort by hand. `config` exits 1 naming all three differences, and the only way to take the new checker and stall is `--force`, which puts `maxRounds` back to 3 and the effort back to the template's. Either the tuning is lost in a diff that looks like routine drift, or the run keeps a stall sized for the old suite.
  - Why it goes to the person: a spec assumption (`plan.md:44`: "`--force` overwrites").
  - Options:
    - (a) Update only the derived keys. Cost: about 15 lines in `cmd_config` (the same three substitutions applied to the existing file, so its formatting is kept), setup-test cases 164-169 rewritten plus one proving a hand-set `maxRounds` survives, the docblock, `README.md` (setup step 2 and the `config` row) and `skills/setup/SKILL.md` step 7. Other differences are listed under `differs` and left alone, exit 0; `--force` stays as the full reset. Effect: a rerun takes the new checker and stall and keeps the person's tuning.
    - (b) Keep `--force` and say what it resets. Cost: two sentences, in the skill's step 7 and the README row. Effect: no code; the trade-off stays but is visible before the person says yes.
    - (c) Accept. Cost: none. Effect: the skill's "only on a yes rerun with `--force`" says nothing of what `--force` drops.
  - Shaper's recommendation: (a). The spec's own reason for exiting 1, "a hand-changed `maxRounds` is the person's", is the reason to keep it on an update, and `unattended --force` already patches only its fill-ins.
  - Decided: (a) Update only the derived keys, with reviewer kept as the person's, 2026-10-23, by @michaelfromtheoutfit (see Decisions). Of the three substitutions the option named, `reviewer` is not applied to an existing file; only `checker` and `checkerStallMs` are.
- **R1-8: The version precheck's permission prompt.**

  - Options:
    - (a) A narrow rule.
